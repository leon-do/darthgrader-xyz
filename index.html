The equation behind the grades

Grades live on one scale where a step is one route grade (4a = 0.5, 4a+ = 1.5, 4b = 2.5…). For two sections g1 then g2 separated by a rest, with gap d = g2 − g1, the pair is worth:

g = g1 + R(d)
R(d) = d + e0·(1 − d/c)p for 0 ≤ d < c
R(d) = e0·(1 + d/cL)q for −cL < d < 0
R(d) = d for d ≥ c, and 0 for d ≤ −cL

e0 is the bonus for two equal sections; c and cL are how far above and below the first grade the second section still matters; p and q shape the fall-off. With more than two sections, the result of each pair becomes g1 of the next.

A boulder after another section

A boulder section u (4A = 0, 4A+ = 1…) counts as u + 3.8 on the route scale, whether it comes first or not. After another section it follows the same equation with larger constants, because it is easier to be pumped after a boulder than to boulder when pumped:

  • Multi-pitch tab: pitches combine like route sections after a good rest; the result is shown in brackets.
  • Soft / hard: g ∓ 0.35. Slash: the mean of the two grades.
  • Result: grade = ⌊g⌋; the fraction g − ⌊g⌋ ≤ 0.25 reads “soft”, above 0.75 “hard”, and within 0.05 of a boundary a slash such as 7a/7a+.
